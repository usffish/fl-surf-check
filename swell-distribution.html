What a normal Florida surf day actually looks like

Every rarity score in the tool is a position in this distribution: pooled daily-maximum swell across all 26 spots, in the ±14-day window around late August, drawn from the marine record that begins 1 October 2021.

Swell height

The shape is the whole story: a hard wall at the left, a long thin tail to the right. Florida is small nearly all the time, and occasionally — when a tropical system lines up — it very much isn't. Half of all days sit between roughly 0.9 and 1.9 ft.

Hover any bar for its range and count. Dashed rules mark percentiles; these are the same numbers the tool reports as VS NORM.

Why the tool measures standard deviations in log space

That long tail breaks a plain standard deviation. On the raw values the mean lands at the 64th percentile rather than the 50th, and the biggest day in five years sits at +5.9 σ — which, at 60 minutes of earned driving per σ, would have justified a six-hour trip. Taking logs first collapses the skew from 1.95 to 0.27 and σ recovers its textbook meaning.

Swell period

Period is the better-behaved of the two — near-symmetric at skew 0.11, centred on 7.15 s. It carries 30% of the quality score because it separates organised groundswell from local wind chop.
